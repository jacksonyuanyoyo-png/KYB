## 1. Overview

Opening and managing complex entity accounts requires careful consideration, precise documentation, and a clear understanding of client needs. Whether you are assisting a new or existing client, determining the appropriate account type, or navigating the requirements for multi-layered corporate structures, this guide is designed to support you. Within these pages, you will find high-level overviews, specific guidance by client type, documentation checklists, and quick-start resources to streamline your process. Use this document as your comprehensive reference for successfully handling complex entity accounts with confidence and efficiency.

## 3. FCC’s complex account pre-review service

The Pre-Review subtype of the Manual Account Opening request type in uDirect can be utilized when requesting FCC Compliance to conduct a review of the account documentation to ensure it has been completed correctly prior to obtaining client signature(s). This eliminates back and forth after client signature has been obtained, providing the best experience for your client. This subtype should be used exclusively for client types that are considered complex.

Refer to the below list for a comprehensive overview of all client types considered as complex include quick reference links to document checklists for all.

**Complex client types & document checklists:**

- Associations, Unions, or Fraternal Organizations: Document checklist available
- Estates: Document checklist
- Graduated Rate Estate: Document checklist
- Multi-layered Corporation — Checklists available for the following scenarios:
  - Corporation Directly Owned By a Corp
  - Corporation Directly Owned By a Trust
- Condominium Corporation: Document checklist
- Non-Profit, Foundation or Charity if not operating as a corporation (no CRA BIN): Document checklist
- Partnerships and Limited Partnerships, Investment Clubs — high-level information available on the New Account Matrix, submit a pre-review request in uDirect for guidance for specific scenarios
  - Partnership document checklist
  - Investment Club document checklist
- Pooled Funds — Information available in the New Account Matrix
- Retirement Compensation Arrangements (RCA) + Personal Pension Plan (PPP) + Individual Pension Plan (IPP): Document checklist
- Trusts directly or indirectly owned or controlled by another entity (incl. bare trusts) — Checklists for the following scenarios available:
  - Formal Trust Directly Owned By A Formal Trust
  - Formal Trust Directly Owned By A Corporation
  - Formal Trust Where Trustee Is An Entity
- First Nations (based on Government of Canada Funding Agreement) (Indigenous Groups): Document checklist

When using FCC’s complex account opening pre-review service via uDirect, you can expect to receive detailed feedback on submitted documentation calling out any gaps or additional information required. Once the review is complete and feedback provided, it is your responsibility to remediate any deficiencies or items that require clarification.

### 4.1 What to do if you don’t know what Manual Account Opening subtype to use?

| Registration type on the form | Subtype to use |
| --- | --- |
| Partnership | Partnership, Limited Partnership |
| Charity/Not for Profit | Non-profit, Charity, Foundation |
| Charity/Not for Profit | Associations, Unions, or Fraternal Organizations |
| Estate | Estates |
| Individual Pension Plan/Retirement Compensation Arrangement | Formal Trust, IPP’s RCA’s PPP’s |

The Client Account Application, section 1 (Client Registration Details), asks you to check one:

- New Client or Update Existing Client (Client ID is mandatory for existing clients)
- Registration Type: Corporation, Trust, Partnership, Charity/Not for Profit, Estate, or Individual Pension Plan/Retirement Compensation Arrangement

If a determination on the subtype is still not clear use the subtype of Associations, Unions, Other.

**Note:** Not using the appropriate subtype might impact processing turnaround as it requires additional review by FCC.

## 6. Glossary of terms

### Beneficial Ownership

Beneficial owners are the individuals who are the trustees, settlor, and known beneficiaries of a trust, or who directly or indirectly own or control 25% or more of i) the shares of a corporation or ii) an entity other than a corporation or trust, such as a partnership. Where the direct beneficial owner is another entity, it is necessary to look through the ownership structure to identify the natural persons who indirectly own or control the entity.

- FINTRAC - Beneficial Ownership Requirements
- CIRO IDPC Rules (3203 - 3204)
- CIRO - Updated Guidance – Investment Dealer Anti-Money Laundering Compliance Guidance (GN-3200-23-001)

### Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA)

Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA) is a federal law that requires all non-US foreign financial institutions to report foreign assets held by a US tax resident (US resident, or citizen) to the Canadian government who in turn report to the IRS under an Intergovernmental Agreement. FATCA was implemented to combat perceived offshore tax evasion.

- CRA Guidance on FATCA
  - Passive Non-financial foreign entity (“NFFE”) – section 4.5
  - Active NFFE description - section 4.6
  - Exempt beneficial Owner - section 3.55, 3.56
  - Estates – sections 9.54 - 9.56
  - Controlling persons - sections 9.72 - 9.80
    - Discretionary beneficiary – section 9.76 - 9.77

### Common Reporting Standards (CRS)

CRS is a standard for the automatic exchange of financial account information developed by the Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development (OECD). Canadian Financial institutions are required to report foreign assets held by a non-Canadian/US tax resident to the Canadian government who in turn report to each jurisdiction’s competent authority. CRS was implemented to combat perceived offshore tax evasion.

- Passive NFFE – section 4.4
- Active NFFE description - section 4.5
- Prescribed non-reporting financial institutions - section 3.5
- Estates – section 9.58 - 9.60
- Controlling persons - sections 9.52 - 9.58
  - Discretionary beneficiary – sections 9.54 – 9.55

- FATCA FFI List (GIIN lookup)
- OECD - Foreign Tax Identification Numbers - by tax jurisdiction and confirms if jurisdiction issues a FTIN and schema

### Qualified Intermediary (QI)

A qualified intermediary is any foreign intermediary (or foreign branch of a U.S. intermediary) that has entered into a qualified intermediary withholding agreement with the IRS. FCC is a withholding QI and has assumed primary withholding and reporting responsibility (10422 (non-US) and 10999 (US)). QI documentation is based on the same requirements set out in the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and regulations and CIRO rules 3202 - 3222. There are two distinct aspects of QI that are important:

1. Document for QI (IRS W-8BEN-E or W-8IMY or where a formation documentation)
2. Treaty claim for reduced withholding on US source income

FCC expects that all clients are documented for QI purposes at time of account opening or within 30 days of account opening. Where QI documentation is not received at time of account opening the account will be systematically restricted within 30 days of account opening (restriction code 56) prohibiting any new buys.

- IRS - Miscellaneous qualified intermediary information
- United States Income Tax Treaties - confirms countries that have entered into a QI Agreement with the IRS where residents of the country are eligible to make a treaty claim for reduced withholding on US source income
- United States - Canada Income Tax Convention
  - Limitation on Benefits Provision - Article XXIX-A
  - Exempt Organizations - Article XXI
- Attachment for Canada - Acceptable QI documentation for entities section 4(ii)
- IRS - US Trust Type Descriptions
- IRS Tax-exempt Organization Search - Typically, only entities that have received IRS section 301(c) status and appear on this list would be considered a tax-exempt entity or foundation for QI/Chapter 3 purposes

## 7. Common entity client updates: Documentation requirements

Find below a summary of required documentation for common entity client updates and information on any additional requirements.

### Updates to Director(s)

- Director Listing

Note that where there are no beneficial owners associated with the entity classified as a Passive NFFE for FATCA and CRS purposes the controlling persons are the natural persons who hold the positions of senior managing officials, such as the chief executive officer or the person who performs that function. It may be necessary to also update the Declaration of Tax Residence for Entities - Part XVII and Part XIX of the Income Tax Act (RC519).

### Updates to Beneficial Owner(s)/Partners/Member(s) of an Investment Club

- Beneficial Owner Identification form
- Declaration of Tax Residence for Entities - Part XVII and Part XIX of the Income Tax Act (RC519) where the entity is classified as a Passive NFFE however in the case of a discretionary beneficiary note that it is only necessary to disclose a discretionary beneficiary in the calendar year that the beneficiary receives a distribution from the trust
- Supporting documentation to evidence the additional or removal of a partner or member of an investment club

### Updates to Trustee(s)

- Supporting documentation to evidence the appointment of the new trustee(s) and the resignation/removal of any existing trustee(s)
- Third-party Identification form for each new trustee
- Valid ID or alternate acceptable form(s) of ID verification for each trustee as outlined by FINTRAC
- Beneficial Owner Identification form
- Declaration of Tax Residence for Entities - Part XVII and Part XIX of the Income Tax Act (RC519) where the entity is classified as a Passive NFFE

### Updates to Signing Officer(s) or a Trading Agent of a Corporate Resolution or similar entity

- Corporate Resolution
- Third-party Identification form where trading agent is being updated
- Valid ID or alternate acceptable form(s) of ID verification for each trustee as outlined by FINTRAC
